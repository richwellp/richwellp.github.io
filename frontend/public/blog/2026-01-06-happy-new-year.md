# Happy New Year!

Firstly, I think this website is close to being maintanable and will no longer undergo drastic changes, unless a new technology/feature or idea come up. I am very content with the current contents of this website and I am happy that I finally created a website worthy of showing others. Thanks to claude code for providing an amazing skeleton code. I have used LLMs to help me understand and implement this website. I even kept notes on the README.md files on how to maintain and update this website. 

The purpose of this blog post is the write a reflection about my decision to pursue Computer Science. 

## Introduced to Computer Science

What do you want to be when you grow up? A dentist. Soldier. Pilot. Astronaut. These what I always said when I was growing up just because they were cool.

My interest began when I was in 7th grade (2012), when I saw my older brother and relatives doing fancy computer stuff that I could not comprehend but thought it was cool. My cousin made me type my Facebook password, and he was able to unblur my Facebook password only for the witnesses to see that my password was just my name.

The Philippines has slow internet speeds. My brother would always download us shows through the internet, and he built a website project for viewing and listing anime shows. The internet at the time was still emerging, and I thought it was really cool (x2).

With these experience, Computer Science became my (realistic) placeholder for what I wanted to be when I grow up. 

## Entering Highschool

My parents migrated to the United States in 2012, and by 2013, my siblings and I moved as well to Illinois. Around this age is when kids are asked to make decisions that will shape their future in school. I was truly aimless and was never a good student. Migrating to the US was a chance for me to be a different person. I wanted to be good in school and smarter. I went through cringe stages or 8th grade syndrome, and I was like that until I had fully matured. I was severely socially anxious. 

We were asked to find jobs and research career prospects in school. I was always asked to consider becoming a Nurse, Anesthesiologist or anything in the healthcare field that makes a lot of money. Life has always been about chasing money. I also discovered software engineers were paid really well especially in San Fransisco but cost of living were intense. Living in Champaign, Illinois, I also realized that UIUC is a great school for Computer Science.

I was split between nursing or computer science (and astronaut because it was cool).

## Tech Advances

We had an old windows PC when I was in elementary and was only using it to play games. Then I was introduced to the internet around 5th grade, exploring games, watching youtube, browsing random websites, playing and chatting on facebook. The Computer AI I experienced back then were purely script based and were easy to beat and predict. 

As you may have guessed, this is when my view completely changed when I witnessed OpenAI beat pro players in Dota 2 on a 1v1. If I only had capital, I would have invested in AI! (Note: I didn't know NVDIA would be the leader today) OpenAI kept improving and even won against 5v5. I was boggled by the AI performance in game. Hence, my interest in AI began. 

## What to do after highschool?

Of course I was not an amazing student but could at least comprehend mathematics. Knowing that UIUC was an amazing school for CS, I gave my best shot to be admitted at the school one day. I kept getting beat by college exams, about to exhaust my free attempts on ACTs and SATs. I never had amazing scores. I could not excel in English section. I was only good with math and never took advanced courses. I felt that I was behind with my classmates who share the dream of getting to prestigious schools. I only took AP Physics and AP Computer Science. **Comparison is the thief of joy.**

### But why do I even want to pursue Computer Science? 

I already had an internal fallback plan of pursuing nursing if I could not get in and fail. 

In my AP Physics class, I was in the same group project with a classmate who shares the same goal as I do, and I asked the same question.

"I want to make the world a better place." He said.

In my head, I was singing "Heal the World" by Michael Jackson.

I couldn’t help but feel a mixture of awe and uncertainty. Could one person really change the world? Could I make a difference too?

**I want to make the world a better place too!**
